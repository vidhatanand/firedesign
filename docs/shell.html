Frame, bands and marks

The page is drafted, not boxed: a centred column with 1px rails, cut into bands by hairlines that run edge to edge. Where a hairline crosses a rail, a small crosshair marks the joint.

data-pad="md" · default

24px above and below. The first band has no top line; the top bar draws it.

data-pad="sm" · data-surface="surface"

16px: toolbars, filters, pagination. A white band holds tables and inputs.

data-pad="lg"

48px: empty states and anything that needs room to breathe.

data-marks="none"

The hairline stays, the crosshairs go. Use it where marks would crowd a dense area.

data-pad="none"

The band's children own their padding, like this split.

Side panel

  • Lines are exactly 1px of --fd-line-faint; marks are 9px of --fd-line-strong, centred on the intersection, so both follow the theme.
  • Add data-grow to the last band so the rails reach the bottom of short pages. On phones the rails and marks drop away and only the hairlines remain.

Page hero

Top-level screens open with a tall title band. The art slot on the right fades toward the text and holds an ASCII texture; keep the title to the screen's name and the line below to what it is for.

Rules

Do

Separate the sections of a page with bands. Use a card only when the thing inside is an object: a plan, a key, a provider.

Don't

Put a second primary button in the top bar, or colour nav items other than the current one. The sidebar is the quietest thing on the screen.
