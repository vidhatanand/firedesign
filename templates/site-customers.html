Fourteen scrapers became three API calls.

Quarry Labs builds price indexes from 9,000 retail sites. In six weeks its data team moved every scraper to Ember, and stopped repairing them.

By Mara Okafor, head of data · Oct 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Quarry Labs sells weekly price indexes to retailers and the analysts who follow them. Every night its crawlers read product and listing pages on 9,000 sites, and every Monday the index ships.

Until this summer those crawlers were 14 scrapers the team had written over four years: a headless browser fleet, a proxy pool and a parser per site. They worked. Keeping them working was the job.

A redesign a week

A retailer that changed its product page broke a parser, and the index missed that retailer until someone fixed it. In the first half of 2026 that happened 31 times. The on-call engineer spent most of the week reading alerts and patching selectors.

Pages, not parsers

The team tried Ember on the three retailers that broke most often. /extract read their product pages with one schema and returned the same fields after two of them redesigned in the trial month.

That settled it. The move took six weeks, from Jul 7 to Aug 15, and one engineer did most of it.

The move

Six weeks, four steps.

  1. Map each scraper to one call

    Ten scrapers only walked listing pages. Each became one /crawl per retailer, limited to the paths that hold products.

  2. Describe the fields once

    The four that parsed product pages became one /extract call with a shared schema: name, price, currency and stock.

  3. Watch prices instead of re-reading pages

    Nightly re-crawls of pages that rarely change became /monitor checks. A webhook arrives only when a price moves.

  4. Run both, then switch off the fleet

    For two weeks both systems ran side by side. When the prices matched, the browsers went off on Aug 15.

What changed

The 215 prices that differed were Ember’s to win: the old scrapers read the page before a sale price loaded. The index has not missed a retailer since the move.

Crawl spend fell from $19,100 in July to $12,600 in October. The larger change is where the team’s week goes.
