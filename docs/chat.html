Components

Chat

One conversation for the AI and for people. Ember AI answers first in plain prose beside a spark; when it needs a person it hands over in the same thread, and a person speaks from a white bubble beside their face. Messages carry real components, not only text.

Anatomy

A head that says who you are talking to, an optional incident strip, a body with one view at a time, and the composer pinned to the bottom. This is the whole chat written by hand; an empty .fd-chat is filled with the same default parts by chat.js.

  • Head (fd-chat-head, 52px): the mark or avatar of who is answering, the title, a status line that changes with them, and icon actions. Under it, an optional fd-chat-banner for incidents.
  • Body (fd-chat-body): views home, conversation, history and offline; one is visible and they slide 16px and fade as they change. The thread is fd-chat-log (role="log", polite).

Four modes

The same chat docks to the edge, floats above its launcher, fills a page or sits beside it. Each specimen below lives inside its box with data-contained; without it, panel and popup position against the viewport, and under 640px every floating mode becomes a full-screen sheet.

Panel

The default: 420px, full height, docked to the right with a soft shadow. It slides in from the edge and covers the page, so the page keeps its layout. Use it for help from anywhere in the console.

Popup

A 400px card, radius 20, that grows out of the launcher’s corner (scale from 96%, a 12px rise and a fade) and closes faster than it opens. The launcher turns into a chevron while it is open. Use it on marketing pages and docs, where a panel would feel heavy.

Inline

Fills its container and is always open: an embedded help page, or a full-page inbox like the support template. Set the height with --fd-chat-h; above 720px the thread keeps a reading width.

Side

A persistent 400px column that pushes the page instead of covering it, for long sessions where the person works and asks at the same time (the playground, a run in progress).

  • Expand (data-expanded) widens a panel to 760px and makes a popup taller and wider. It is hidden on phones, where the chat is already full screen.
  • On phones the chat is modal: focus stays inside, the page behind stops scrolling, and the composer keeps clear of the home indicator (env(safe-area-inset-bottom)).
  • Open state, mode and size are remembered for the browser session. Contained chats opt in with data-persist; any chat opts out with data-persist="false".

Launcher, badge and teaser

The launcher is the floating action button. Its chat icon turns and fades into a chevron while the chat is open; an unread count pops in with the spring ease. A teaser offers help once, after a delay, and remembers a dismissal for the session.

Need help with your crawl?Ember AI can read your last run.
  • data-fd-chat-toggle="<id>" on any button makes it a launcher: aria-expanded and aria-controls stay in sync, and closing the chat returns focus to it. Add data-float to pin it to the bottom-right corner.
  • The teaser (.fd-chat-teaser[data-for][data-delay]) rises in after the delay unless the chat is open. Say one useful thing; never two teasers on one page.
  • setUnread(n) updates every launcher for that chat and its accessible name (“Chat with support, 2 unread”). Opening the chat clears it.

Home, history and offline

Home is where a new conversation starts: a greeting with one accent word, a muted line on what the assistant can do, and up to four questions as right-aligned outlined pills that send themselves. History lists past conversations with their state; offline takes a message when nobody is in.

  • The “Continue conversation” row appears on Home while a conversation is open, with the last line, its time and an unread dot.
  • History search filters as you type and shows an empty state that says what to try. Badges: Open (info), Waiting (warning), Resolved (neutral); a dot marks unread replies.
  • The offline form is a form block: it validates, then folds into a short summary with an Edit link.

  • Messages from the same author within two minutes form a run: the avatar and name show once, the gap drops to 4px, and the bubble corner on the speaker’s side tightens from 16 to 6. chat.js sets data-continued from data-time.
  • Times show once per run in the head; on any other message they appear on hover or focus. Hover or focus a message for its actions: copy, react, reply, and for the AI, good and bad answer. Five reactions render as chips under the message.
  • System lines are small, centred and muted between hairlines: dates, joins, handovers, resolution. The unread divider is the one in accent.
  • New messages rise 8px and fade in. When you have scrolled up, a “New messages” pill appears instead of moving the thread under you.

Rich blocks

A message body is a stack of blocks, <div class="fd-chat-block" data-type="…">, each built from an existing Fire component. Every specimen below is rendered by chat.render() from the JSON beside it, and every one works: click, choose, submit.

1 · Text

Prose at chat density, 14/22. Supports paragraphs, lists, `code`, **bold**, links and [1] citations, or pass html.

JSON
{
  "author": "ai",
  "time": "2026-11-14T14:20:00",
  "blocks": [{
    "type": "text",
    "text": "Crawls charge **one credit per page**, so a `limit` is also a budget.\n\n- Start at the docs root\n- Add `includePaths` to stay in one section\n- See [crawl options](https://docs.ember.dev/crawl-options)"
  }]
}

2 · Quick replies

Offer the next likely answer. Choosing one sends it as your message and the row folds away so it can’t be used twice; multi collects several with a Confirm. Never more than three.

JSON
[{
  "author": "ai",
  "text": "Did raising the timeout fix it?",
  "blocks": [{ "type": "quick-replies",
    "options": ["That fixed it", "Still failing", "Talk to a person"] }]
}, {
  "author": "ai",
  "text": "Which endpoints does your team call most?",
  "blocks": [{ "type": "quick-replies", "multi": true, "confirm": "Confirm",
    "options": ["/scrape", "/crawl", "/agent"] }]
}]

  • chat.demo(el, 'ai' | 'support' | 'handoff') plays a script; handoff renders the first part at once and plays from the refund. With reduced motion the final state renders immediately.
  • Chats with data-fd-chat-demo also answer free questions from a small script: failed requests, credits, invoices, rate limits, refunds and “talk to a person”.

JavaScript

fire/js/chat.js wires every chat on load and exports the controller. It reuses the agent family for the composer, streaming and tool calls, and code, data, forms, charts and overlays for what blocks contain.

open({ trigger }) · close() · toggle()Floating modes only. Opening animates from the launcher, clears unread and focuses the composer; closing returns focus to the launcher.

show(view)home, conversation, history or offline, sliding forward or back.

add(message) · update(id, patch) · remove(id)Add a message object (see below) or element; patch state, text, html, blocks, time or edited; remove with a fade.
setTyping(author | false, opts)One typing indicator: 'human' (face and dots) or 'ai' (shimmering opts.label).
stream(id, text, { speed })Streams text or HTML into a new text block of that message with the caret. Returns a promise.
setAgent({ kind, name, role, avatar, status, ahead })kind: ai, human, away or queue. Updates the head and the disclaimer.
setUnread(n) · setDisabled(reason | false)Launcher badges and accessible names; the closed state of the composer.
addFiles(files) · newConversation()Attach files as chips with upload progress; clear the thread and return to Home.
chat.render(message)Builds a message element from data.
chat.demo(el, name) · chat.stopDemo(el)Plays or stops a scripted conversation.
chat.setStep · setQueue · resolveConfirm · citeDrive a steps block, count a handoff queue down, resolve an approval card yourself, write a citation chip.

Rules

Do

Keep the AI and people visibly different: the AI is prose beside a spark with an “AI” mark; a person has a face, a name, a role and a bubble. Say in the thread when the speaker changes.

Don't

Give the AI a human name or face, or let a person’s message look like the AI’s. Never hide that a handoff happened.

Do

Offer at most three quick replies, phrased as the person would say them (“Was I charged for it?”), and let them fold away once used.

Don't

Use quick replies as a menu of everything the assistant can do, or ask the same question twice.

Do

Ask before anything that moves money or changes access, with the amount and destination in the title, and resolve the card in place.

Don't

Put more than one primary button in a message, or show a rating before the conversation is over.

Do

Show what the AI looked up as a compact tool call, and cite pages with numbered chips and a sources row.

Don't

Use emoji as icons or reactions, or move the thread while someone is reading further up.
