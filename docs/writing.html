Foundations

Writing

The words on a screen: labels, buttons, descriptions, help, empty states, errors and agent messages. Write only what the layout does not already say, and make every word a fact.

The idea

The screen is the explanation. Words are what is left over. Before you write a sentence, ask whether the layout could say it instead.

  • A filter that shows its current value (“Last 7 days”) needs no label saying “Date range”.
  • A disabled Save button says “nothing has changed” without a note.
  • A figure with its unit (“1,520 credits”) needs no sentence about what it measures.
  • An example already typed in a field says more than a paragraph about the format.

Three habits

  • One job per element. A title names the thing. A description says what you do with it. A hint says what happens. A button says the action.
  • Say it once. If the title says “API keys”, the description does not begin “API keys are…”.
  • Put detail one step away. What a person needs to act stays on the screen. What they might want to know goes behind a tooltip, a link or the docs.

Method

Six steps for any piece of copy. It takes a minute and it is the whole method.

  1. Write what the person needs to decide or doOne plain sentence, as if telling a colleague. Do not try to be short yet.
  2. Delete what the screen already showsThe page title, the section, the field's label, the value in the field.
  3. Delete what they already knowThat forms are filled in, that buttons are clicked, that saving saves.
  4. Replace every adjective with a fact“Fast” becomes “about 2 seconds”. “Soon” becomes “Nov 23”.
  5. Move the rest awayBackground, edge cases and reasons go to a tooltip, a link or the docs.
  6. Read what is left aloudIf you would not say it to someone sitting next to you, rewrite it.

Page head

A title and one line. The line starts with a verb and says what this page is for.

Write

Activity logs

Every request your keys made, with its status and credits.

Not

Welcome to the Activity Logs Page

Welcome to the Activity Logs page, where you can take a look at your recent request activity.

  • The title is 1 to 3 words and names the place. The line is up to 12 words.
  • The line does not welcome anyone, and it does not describe the product.

Sections and settings

A short title, then one sentence that says what the thing is for or what changing it does. If the sentence only repeats the title, delete it.

Write

Team slug

Used in your team's URLs. Leave blank to use the team ID.

Zero data retention

Ember stores no page content or request bodies for this team.

Not

Team slug

The team slug is the slug for your team.

Zero data retention

Enable this option to turn on zero data retention mode.

  • The description says what it is for, or what changing it does. It never restates the title.
  • Do not tell a person to “enable this option”. The switch already says so.
  • A section title is 1 to 3 words. A description is one sentence of up to 14 words.

Buttons

A verb, or a verb and its object. The label says what happens when it is pressed, so a person can decide without reading anything else.

Write
Not
  • Prefer the specific verb: “Create key”, “Export CSV”, “Copy link”, “Start crawl”, “Revoke”.
  • One word is enough when the place gives the object: “Save”, “Run”, “Install”, “Delete”.
  • Never “OK”, “Submit”, “Yes”, “Click here”, or “Continue” unless it truly is a step in a sequence.
  • In a dialog, the primary button repeats the verb of the title: “Delete team?” has “Delete team”, not “Confirm”.

Fields

A label names the value, a placeholder shows a real example, a hint carries one fact, an error says what to change.

Write

Shown on invoices.

This name is taken. Try quarry-labs-2.

Not

This is the name your team will be known by, and you can change it at any time in your settings.

Invalid input

  • Label: the name of the value, as a noun, in 1 to 3 words. Not a question and not an instruction.
  • Placeholder: an example that could be real. Never the label again, never “Enter…”.
  • Hint: only when a careful person could still get it wrong. One fact: the effect, the default, the limit or an example.
  • Error: what is wrong and what to change, in the field's own terms. Never “Invalid input”.

Filters and controls that hold a value

Show the current value as the label. The control then needs no separate caption, and the screen reads as a sentence about what is being shown.

Write
Not
  • The label is the value: “All endpoints”, “Last 7 days”, “Format: Markdown”, “All API keys (3)”.
  • A control that names the question instead of the answer needs a caption to make sense.

Figures and stat tiles

A short label, the figure with its unit, and at most one small qualifier that gives the figure meaning: the comparison, the limit or the date.

Write

Failed runs None All quiet this week
Not
The number of credits you have left to use 1,520 This is how many credits are still available on your plan this month
Failed runs - Nothing to show
  • The label counts something in 1 to 3 words. The figure carries its unit.
  • One qualifier at most: “12.4% more than last month”, “of 3,000, resets Nov 23”.
  • When the figure is zero or missing, say so in words: “0 active”, “All quiet”, “Never”, “None”. Not a dash.

Empty states

Three kinds, written differently. Each says what will appear and how to make it appear. Title up to 5 words, one sentence, one action.

Nothing yet

No monitors yet

Watch a page and get a webhook when it changes.

Nothing matches

No activity in this range

Try a different date range, endpoint or API key.

Nothing possible

Logs are off

Zero data retention is on for this team, so requests are not stored.

Not

Oops! There is nothing here

Sorry, it looks like you have not created any monitors yet. Don't worry, you can get started by clicking the button below!

  • Name the kind first. First use invites the action, a filter that matches nothing offers to clear it, and a blocked feature says why and where to fix it.
  • Do not apologise, do not say “Oops”, and do not describe the emptiness (“There is nothing here”).
  • Say what will appear (“a webhook when it changes”) and how to make it appear.

Loading, progress and success

Name what is happening, with the object, in the present tense. When it is done, say what is now true.

Write
Parsing page 3 of 12

Key created

Not
Loading…

Your API key was successfully created!

  • Name the work and its object: “Searching the web”, “Reading 4 pages”, “Parsing page 3 of 12”. Bare “Loading…” is for when you truly do not know.
  • Where the wait has a length, say it: “About 20 seconds left”.
  • Success says what is now true: “Key created”, “Copied”, “Invite sent to dana@quarry.dev”. Drop “successfully”: if it had failed, you would be showing an error.

Errors

An error answers two questions: what happened, and what should I do. Lead with the first, end with the second.

Write

Couldn't reach docs.example.com

Check the URL, or try again in a minute. Request ID req_8f2a

You're out of credits

They reset Nov 23, or upgrade to keep going.

Not

Something went wrong

Please try again later.

Error 402: Payment Required

  • Say what happened, then what to do. Include whatever a person needs to get help, such as a request ID in mono.
  • Never blame the person (“You entered an invalid URL”).
  • Never show a raw code as the whole message. The code can follow, in mono.

Confirmations

Only confirm what cannot be undone or costs money. The title is the action as a question, the body says exactly what will be lost, and the button repeats the verb.

Write

Delete Quarry Labs?

This removes the team, its 3 API keys and 30 days of logs. It can't be undone.

Not

Are you sure?

Are you sure you want to proceed with this action? Please confirm that you wish to continue.

  • The body names what will be lost, with numbers: “3 API keys and 30 days of logs”.
  • The primary button repeats the verb of the title: “Delete team”, never “Confirm”.
  • If the action can be undone, do it and offer “Undo” in a toast instead of asking first.

Callouts and banners

A callout interrupts, so it is only for something the person must know now: a limit about to be hit, a failed payment, a setting with a side effect.

Write

90% of credits used

At this rate you'll run out around Nov 21.

Not

Important notice regarding your account usage

Please note that you have now used up 90% of the credits that are included in your current plan. If you would like to avoid any interruption to your service, you may want to consider upgrading.

  • One title of up to 5 words, up to two sentences, at most one action.
  • Standing information belongs in a description or a hint, not a callout. A screen with three callouts has none.

Tooltips

Two uses only: the name of an icon-only control, and a short definition of a term. Up to 8 words.

Write
Copy key
ZDR: zero data retention
Not
Click this button to copy your API key to the clipboard
You must paste your new key somewhere safe before closing this window
  • A tooltip names an icon-only control (“Copy key”) or defines a term (“ZDR: zero data retention”).
  • It never carries anything a person must read to finish the task. It is invisible on touch screens and to anyone not hovering.

Help text

The pull is always to add a sentence “to be safe”. Most of the time the sentence makes the screen worse. Start at the top of the ladder.

Add help only when

  • A careful first-time user would probably get it wrong.
  • The consequence is money, security, or something that cannot be undone.
  • The term is ours, not theirs, and cannot be renamed.

Fix the label before you add help

Write
Not

A slug is the part of a URL that identifies your team. It can only contain lowercase letters, numbers and dashes, and it appears in every link you share.

  • A field called “Slug” with a two-line explanation is better as “URL name” with none.
  • Where long text is right. Money, security, legal terms and irreversible actions deserve complete sentences, because a short version would mislead.
  • Even then: short sentences, the consequence first, numbers instead of adjectives, and set as quiet small text beside the thing it explains. Not a headline, not a banner.

Voice and mechanics

One voice across the product, and a few fixed rules for how text is set.

Voice

  • Plain. The word a person would say. “Use”, not “utilise”. “Start”, not “initiate”. “Stop”, not “terminate”.
  • Specific. Numbers, names, dates, examples: “3 keys”, “Nov 23”, “about 2 seconds”.
  • Direct. Second person, active voice, present tense: “You have 1,520 credits”.
  • Calm. No exclamation marks, no “Oops”, no “Awesome”, no “Please” or “Sorry” as padding. One “sorry” is right when we broke something.
  • Honest. Say what will happen, including the cost, before it happens: “Costs 1 credit.”
  • The user's terms. Endpoints, keys, credits, requests and status codes, in mono where they are literal. Do not invent product names for ordinary things.

Mechanics

  • Case. Sentence case everywhere: “API keys”, “Activity logs”, “Create key”. Uppercase only for mono eyebrows and stat labels, which the styles do for you.
  • Full stops. None on titles, labels, buttons, badges, table headers and tooltips. Descriptions, hints, errors and empty-state lines are sentences and end with one.
  • Numbers. Digits, with thousands separators and the unit: “1,520 credits”, “842 ms”, “3 of 12”. Tabular figures in tables.
  • Dates and times. Short and unambiguous: “Nov 23”, “Feb 10, 2026 4:29 PM”. Relative for the recent past: “2 minutes ago”. Say the time zone once where it matters: “Dates use UTC.”
  • Identifiers. Keys, IDs, paths, endpoints, file names and code are mono. Never translate or reword them.
  • Lists in a sentence. “PDF, DOCX and XLSX”.
  • Links. The link text says where it goes: “billing settings”, “Webhook security”. Never “click here”.
  • Truncation. Put the important word first. “Monthly limit” survives being cut; “Limit for the month” does not.

One name per thing

Pick the word and use it everywhere: the interface, the docs and the API.

Agent and AI screens

The prompt field shows a task someone could copy. The run says what the agent is doing as it does it.

Composer and suggestions

Not

Run steps

Write
  1. Searched the web2.1s
  2. Reading 4 pages2 of 4 pages6.4s
  3. Comparing prices
Not
  1. Step 12.1s
  2. Loading…6.4s
  3. Processing…
  • The prompt field's placeholder is an example task, concrete enough to copy. Not “Ask me anything”.
  • Suggestions are real requests, each different in kind, each short enough to read at a glance.
  • Say what the agent is doing as it does it: “Searching”, “Reading 4 pages”, “Comparing prices”. Past tense when done: “Read 4 pages”.
  • Show cost and limits before the run, not after: “Costs about 12 credits”, “5 free runs left today”.
  • State uncertainty plainly: “Couldn't find a price for the Enterprise tier.” Not a confident guess, and not an apology.
  • Name sources: the site and the page title, not a description of them.
  • The agent speaks as the product, in the same voice as the rest of the interface. It does not chat, flatter or narrate its feelings.

Checklist

Run these twelve questions on every screen before you ship it.

  1. Can a person tell what this screen is for from the title and one line?
  2. Does every button say what it does, without reading the text around it?
  3. Is anything said twice? Delete one.
  4. Is there a sentence that only restates a label or a title? Delete it.
  5. Is every adjective a fact?
  6. Is every element inside its budget?
  7. Does each empty state say what will appear and offer the action?
  8. Does each error say what happened and what to do?
  9. Is help text in the lightest place that works?
  10. Is the same thing called the same name everywhere?
  11. Sentence case, no stray full stops, digits with units, mono identifiers?
  12. Read the screen aloud, top to bottom. Does it sound like one person explaining it plainly?
  • The full text is in WRITING.md. The rules an AI agent follows are in AGENT-RULES.md.

Translating Fire's labels

Most words on a page are your markup, so you translate them yourself. A few are written by Fire's scripts as people use the page, such as “Show API key” on a secret field. Give those the page's language and they follow it through every interaction.

  • Put data-label-<key> on the component, or on html for the whole page. Dots in the key become hyphens: menu.open is read from data-label-menu-open.
  • For a whole language at once, add one dictionary to the page: <script type="application/json" data-fd-labels>{"menu.open": "Menü öffnen"}</script>. It is data, so a strict Content Security Policy allows it. An attribute beats the dictionary, and the dictionary beats English.
  • {name} marks a value Fire fills in. Keep it in the translation, in the order your language needs.
  • Words you wrote in markup are never replaced. Sample content in these docs, and the Tweaks panel, are not translated.
