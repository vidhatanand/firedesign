Website

Site foundation

The layout every public page stands on: named breakpoints, fluid type, section rhythm, grounds, pictures, the container and one section head. The 15 section types are built on it: header, hero, logos, features, split, stats, steps, quote, pricing, compare, FAQ, call to action, footer, posts and article (Sections).

Breakpoints

Four named widths. Sections measure the .fd-site container, never the viewport, so a site renders the same inside a board or a docs specimen as on its own page.

NameWidthWhat changes
phonebelow 640pxOne column everywhere; actions fill the width; compare shows one plan; the texture scrim becomes bands.
tablet640 to 899pxThe header's links move into the sheet; splits and heads stack; grids drop to two columns.
desktop900px and upEvery variant at its designed layout.
wide1200px and upThe fluid steps reach their ceiling; content stops at 1,200px.
  • The same numbers the app shell and the docs kit use. They are in the contract (breakpoints in contract/fire-contract.json) for tools; markup never names them.
  • data-only="desktop|mobile" keeps its meaning; there is no phone-only or tablet-only attribute.

Fluid type

Four steps that grow with the container between a phone floor and a wide ceiling. They follow data-text-size (rem) and the type set: its display face, weight, tracking and heading case, and a set that steps its headings up or down scales them too.

  • inverse flips the colour scheme inside the section, so every token and component follows: no separate inverse styles.
  • accent sets text, lines and the focus ring in the accent's contrast colour. Its primary action is the inverse button.
  • texture puts an fd-ascii behind the content and a scrim of the page colour under the text; the field shows where there is no text, and in bands above and below it on a phone.

  • Use it for a hero or a closing call to action, once a page at most, and never for a section people read at length.

Questions about credits.

How are credits counted?
A scrape costs 1 credit. A crawl costs 1 credit per page, and /extract adds 4 credits per page for structured output.
Do unused credits roll over?
No. Credits reset on the first of each month, and your spend cap stays where you set it.
Can I cap what a key spends?
Yes. Set a monthly cap per team or per key; requests stop at the cap until credits reset.
Do you store the pages you read?
Logs keep request metadata for 30 days. With zero data retention on, Ember stores no page content at all.
What happens when a page fails?
Ember retries twice with a new browser. A page that still fails costs nothing and shows in your logs with its reason.

Keep a site from looking generated

A large library of website blocks makes every site look the same. Fire ships a grammar instead, and these rules.

Do
  • Vary a site by its look: palette, type set, signature texture and the dials. The same markup under folio and operator should read as two companies.
  • Start-align. Let one thing in each section be the largest, and use the accent once at most.
  • Divide with hairlines and space: ruled cells, a rule above a section.
  • Show the product: a frame holding real Fire components, or the signature texture. Where a picture is the point, let the look treat it (data-imagery).
  • Write facts with units: "842 ms median", "1,000 free credits a month".
Don't
  • Add a decorative variant. Variants differ in structure, never in skin.
  • Centre everything, or put a pill badge above every headline.
  • Use gradient text, glow, glass, drop-shadowed cards in a row of three, or an icon in a tinted square over every feature.
  • Use grey boxes for media, stock avatars, emoji or illustrations, or set text over a picture anywhere but the image ground.
  • Write "supercharge", "seamless", "unlock" or "the future of".
