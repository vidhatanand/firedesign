Components

Charts

Quiet instruments. A hairline grid, mono axes, one accent series and nothing else to look at. They read fast, resize cleanly and tell you the number the moment you point at it.

Line and area

Use a line for a value that moves continuously and an area when you also want the eye to feel its volume. Series 1 is the accent, series 2 is ink. Lines draw in once, the first time they scroll into view.

  • Y labels are compact (1.2k, 3.4M) and right-aligned; x labels thin themselves so they never collide, and tooltips can carry a longer titles entry than the axis label.
  • The area fill is the series colour at 12% fading to nothing. It is the only gradient in the system.
  • Set yFormat to number, compact, percent, ms or credits.

Thresholds and live data

When a number has a limit, draw the limit on the chart and label it where it sits, in mono capitals. A live series ends in a dot with a soft pulsing ring, the only loop in the family.

  • The threshold value is part of the y domain, so the line is never clipped off the top. Add "tone": "danger" to the threshold when crossing it is an error rather than a limit.
  • Hover or press anywhere on the chart: the crosshair snaps to the nearest bucket and the tooltip flips sides before it reaches an edge.

Bars and stacked bars

Bars compare discrete periods. Grouped bars put series side by side; stacked bars show a total and its parts, with the rounded top on the top segment only. Bars grow from the baseline, 12ms apart.

  • A null value is a gap, not a zero: Sunday of this week has not happened yet, so it has no bar and the tooltip shows an en dash.
  • Stacked tooltips add a Total row. Use a status tone (danger for failures) for a series that carries meaning; otherwise leave the slot colours alone.

Legends

A legend is a row of small swatches and muted labels. In a chart it is a set of toggles: click a series to hide it, and the scale adjusts. The last visible series cannot be turned off.

A sparkline is a shape, not a reading: put it beside a figure to show direction. Without a tone it colours itself by trend; add data-invert where lower is better.

Donut and progress ring

A thin ring for parts of a whole, with two to five segments, the total in the centre and the legend beside it. Hover a segment or a legend row to read it in the centre.

  • A progress ring shows one value against a known end: an import, a crawl. A quota or limit is a meter, not a ring: a bar says "how much is left" faster.

Heat grid

Activity over weeks, one cell per day. Five steps run from a faint ink wash to the full accent, cut at the quartiles of the busy days so one spike does not flatten the rest.

  • Pass one number per day from start (any weekday); weeks run Monday to Sunday and a partial week is left empty. Focus the grid and use the arrow keys to walk it: left and right by week, up and down by day.
  • data-size="sm|lg" makes the cells 11px or 18px. They shrink to fit narrow containers and the grid scrolls sideways below that.

States

Loading keeps the chart's height so nothing jumps. A range with no data says so and suggests what to change. A chart with data that is all zeros is not empty: it draws a flat accent line on the baseline.

Chart cards

The standard composition: a title and a muted line on the left, a figure or a range switch on the right, the chart, then the legend. Cards separate one chart from the next; inside them nothing else is boxed.

Sizing

Charts are fluid. They redraw when their container changes width (debounced), keep a fixed height, and drop ticks as space runs out. Below 420px the y axis keeps at most four labels.

Rules

Do

Lead with the accent for the one series that matters and make everything else ink or muted. Keep axes in mono, label thresholds on the line, and show the number on hover.

Don't

Use more than six series, give every series its own hue, or add gradients, shadows, 3D or rounded-blob bars. If a chart needs a legend of eight colours it needs to be two charts.

Do

Use a meter (a bar) for a quota or a limit, and a ranked list for a ranking. Both answer "how much" faster than a ring.

Don't

Use a donut for a quota, or hide the baseline of an empty chart. A flat line on a zero baseline is data; a blank card is a question.
