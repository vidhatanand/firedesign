Foundations

Theming and tweaks

Fire's whole look runs on variables: fifteen palettes recolour every neutral and the accent, tone bends whichever palette you pick, twelve type sets change the voice of the headings and the interface, a handful of scales set density, corners, lines, text size and motion, four structural dials decide what surfaces, shadows, controls and small labels look like, one dial treats a site's pictures, and a signature sets the textures and marks. The Tweaks panel turns all of it into live controls.

Tweaks panel

Use it to find a product's character before you commit to it, or ship it so your users can tune density, contrast and motion for themselves. It floats beside the page and is not modal, so every change can be watched on the real screen.

  • Any element with data-fd-tweaks-open toggles the panel and carries its aria-expanded state. The docs top bar has one.
  • The Looks row at the top applies one of the eight named looks whole and names the active one; any change after it reads Custom. applyLook(name) does the same from code.
  • No trigger of your own? Put data-fd-tweaks on <html> and a round launcher appears bottom-left.
  • Choices are saved in localStorage["fd-tweaks"] (only what differs from the default) and applied as soon as the module runs. Load fire/boot.js as a plain (non-module) script in <head>, before the stylesheet, and the saved look is on <html> before the first paint, so a reload never flashes the default. Reset leaves no attribute or inline style behind.
  • The page's own look is the default. A page that declares data-palette, data-type or a structural dial (data-surfaces, data-elevation, data-controls, data-headings) or data-imagery on <html> keeps it on load and after Reset, and only a choice away from it is saved; a page that declares nothing defaults to Ember and Geist. Before fire/boot.js restores a saved palette or type set it keeps the page's values in data-fd-default-palette and data-fd-default-type, so the panel still knows them; never write those by hand.
  • Copy CSS gives you a block to paste into your product: the attributes for <html> and a :root rule with every overridden variable, toned palette colours included. A type set is just an attribute, so it needs fire/typefaces.css (already part of fire.css).
  • Keyboard: Tab moves between controls, arrows move inside a group (the palette grid is 5 × 3 and the typeface grid 4 × 3), Escape closes the panel and returns focus to its trigger. On desktop the header drags; double-click it to dock again. Under 640px it is a bottom sheet.
JavaScriptDoes
open(trigger) · close() · toggle(trigger)Show or hide the panel. Focus returns to trigger on close.
get()The full state: { palette, type, tone, theme, density, corners, lines, textSize, contrast, motion, texture, surfaces, elevation, controls, headings, imagery, signature }.
set(partial)Merge and apply, for example set({ palette: "paper", type: "editorial", tone: { warmth: 30 } }). Unknown or out-of-range values are ignored or clamped.
reset()Everything back to the page's own look: its declared palette and type set, otherwise Ember and Geist.
toCss()The Copy CSS text for the current state.

It sets the page, surfaces, lines, ink and accent for both themes, so every component inside takes its colour without a line of extra CSS. Pick one to change a product's character without touching a component.

Type sets

The twelve sets are specs, and a look of your own is another. A theme record's type may be a set's name or a spec: up to three families from the catalogue below, plus how headings are weighted, tracked and cased and how figures are set. validateTheme() checks the pairing; expandTheme() writes the font tokens and the fallback faces, so swapping a font does not move the layout.

  • display sets --fd-font-display (headings), sans the interface and mono code, keys and eyebrows. A key left out leaves the default.
  • displayWeight is --fd-weight-display; displayTracking, in em, is the letter-spacing of every heading level.
  • headingCase: "upper" writes data-heading-case="upper" and figures: "tabular" writes data-figures="tabular"; both are in fire/typefaces.css.

The pairing rules

  • At most two families plus a mono: display and sans are the two, mono is the third. They may be one family.
  • The interface face needs an x-height of at least 0.5 of the em, so 13 and 14px text stays legible. Space Grotesk, Hanken Grotesk and Source Sans 3 fall just under, and make good headings.
  • A family marked display-only (Anton, Bebas Neue, Big Shoulders Display, Syne, Unbounded, Doto, Pixelify Sans and the display serifs) is never the interface face, whatever its x-height.
  • The mono must be a mono. A mono may also be the interface face (JetBrains Mono, Geist Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Fira Code, Roboto Mono), as in Terminal and Pixel.
  • displayWeight must be a weight the display family has: a range for a variable family, the listed weights for a static one.
  • A family outside the catalogue is allowed, for a licensed face. Fire cannot check its pairing or match a fallback to it, and says so in a note.

Every family is open-licensed (SIL OFL 1.1) and has a Latin woff2 subset, measured metrics and a fallback face in contract/fonts.json. Filter by what it can do and how it sounds.

FamilyRolesVoicex-heightWeightsLicenceIn Fire

Using a family in a product

Fire's own fire/fonts/ keeps the nineteen families its type sets use. For any other catalogue family, copy or download its files, with the licence, into your product's font folder.

  • Every file is checked against the SHA-256 in the catalogue before it is written; add can be run again for each family a product uses.
  • Each family's fallback is Arial, Times New Roman or Courier New, resized to the web font's average letter width, ascent, descent and line gap. local() cannot name system-ui, so the stack still ends in it.

Tone

Tone bends the chosen palette instead of replacing it: rotate the accent toward your brand, mute it, lift it, or warm the page. At zero every control is exactly the palette. Try one on this page:

ControlRangeWhat it does
hue−180° to 180°Rotates the accent, its hover, its text colour and the primary button's inner shade, in OKLCH, so lightness holds while the colour turns.
vivid0% to 150%Scales the accent's chroma. 0% is a grey accent; above 100% it saturates until the screen's gamut stops it.
light−12 to 12Shifts the accent's lightness by that many hundredths of L.
warmth−100 to 100Tints the page, surfaces, lines, ink and washes toward blue or yellow-orange. At ±100 the page is clearly tinted but still neutral; the whitest surface takes the least.
  • After every bend the engine re-applies the palette rules: accent text stays at 4.5:1 or more on the page, on surfaces and on the accent wash, and the label on the accent keeps its colour while it reaches 4.5:1, switching between white and a near-black tinted to the accent when it would not.
  • Ink on the page stays at 12:1 or more in every palette and theme.
  • A toned palette is written as inline variables on <html>; Copy CSS writes the same values out as a palette you can keep.

Structure

Four dials change how a page is built rather than its colours: what an object is, how things float, how secondary controls look and the voice of small labels. Combine a few values to get a very different product from the same markup and no CSS of your own.

AttributeValuesWhat it changes
data-surfaces(objects) · flat · outlinedObjects are white with a hairline on an off-white page. Flat tints cards, tables and inputs and drops their edges, so fill alone separates them. Outlined gives the page and its objects one colour and draws the edges, with inputs edged at 3:1.
data-elevationnone · (soft) · liftedNone removes every shadow; menus, dialogs and thumbs take an edge instead. Lifted deepens the shadows of floating and raised things and lifts cards, tables and form sections off the page.
data-controls(soft) · outlined · filledThe secondary button, the soft select, the soft search and the filter trigger: the 4% ink wash, an edge at 3:1 on a clear fill, or a stronger wash. The primary button keeps its depth in every value.
data-headings(sentence) · upper · monoThe small labels: eyebrows, column headers, keys, stat labels and group labels. Sentence keeps mono capitals for eyebrows and sans capitals for the sidebar's groups; upper sets them all in tracked sans capitals; mono sets them all in the mono face, as written.

Pictures

One dial treats every picture on a site the way the look wants it, so the same photograph reads as two companies under two looks. It moves only fd-site-media and the image ground (Site foundation).

AttributeValuesWhat it changes
data-imagery(natural) · mono · duotone · washAs shot; greyscale with its contrast eased toward the page; a two-ink print, shadows in the ink and lights in a tint of the accent; the picture's colour turned down under a thin veil of the accent. A picture's own data-treatment wins.
  • The Pictures row sits in the panel's Motion, texture and pictures section; a theme record carries it as dials.imagery, and a page that declares data-imagery on <html> keeps it as its default, as for the structural dials.

Make your own palette

A palette sets twenty tokens (the last, --fd-focus, is the focus ring), each as a light-dark() pair. Start from Copy CSS in the panel (pick the nearest palette, tone it, copy) and rename the block.

  • Page and surfaces: --fd-bg, --fd-bg-sunken, --fd-surface, --fd-surface-raised, --fd-surface-inverse, --fd-scrim, --fd-thumb. Keep surfaces lighter than the page in both themes.
  • Lines: --fd-line-faint, --fd-line, --fd-line-strong.
  • Ink: --fd-ink, --fd-ink-inverse, and the two wash colours. --fd-wash makes every fill and tint (its alpha steps are --fd-a1 to --fd-a24); --fd-ink-wash makes every text step (--fd-a32 to --fd-a88). Give both a little of your neutral hue.
  • Accent: --fd-accent, --fd-accent-hover, --fd-accent-contrast (the label on it), --fd-accent-text (AA on the page), --fd-accent-glow (the primary button's inner shade).
  • Everything else derives by itself on [data-palette]: the ink ladder, text steps, fills, accent washes, the focus ring and the primary button's shadow.

Theme records

A theme record is a product's whole look as one JSON object: the palette, its tone, the type set and the dials. The Tweaks panel, agents and Facet all read and write it, and fire/js/theme.js turns it into tokens. Every field is optional: a missing field is Fire's default, and an unknown field is an error. The schema is contract/theme.schema.json.

FieldDefaultWhat it sets
contract"fire/1"The record format.
palette"ember"A palette name (data-palette), or a spec Fire builds a palette from: an accent, brand colours, or the full spec form of fire/js/palettes.js.
tone0 · 100 · 0 · 0hue, vivid, light and warmth, whole numbers in the ranges of Tone. A toned palette is written out as its twenty colour tokens.
type"geist"A type set name (data-type), or a type set spec: display, sans and mono families from the catalogue, displayWeight, displayTracking, headingCase and figures. See Type sets.
dialscorners 0–2 (--fd-radius-scale), density (data-density), lines 1 · 1.5 · 2 (--fd-hairline), textSize (data-text-size), contrast (data-contrast), motion off · default · slow (--fd-motion), texture 0–1.5 (--fd-texture), and the dials of Structure and Pictures: surfaces, elevation, controls, headings and imagery (data-imagery; the default, natural, writes no attribute).

A palette from an accent

Give one colour and how the neutrals should lean: pure grey, cool, warm, tinted toward the accent (the default) or a hue in degrees. Fire builds the palette on Ember's lightness structure with the same engine as the fifteen presets, so it passes the same checks: text at 4.5:1 on every background, the label at 4.5:1 on the accent and its hover, accent text at 4.5:1 on the page and surfaces. Add "on": "white" or "dark" to ask for a label. When a colour cannot pass, it moves the least it must, and the record's notes say how far and why.

A palette from brand colours

Give one to three brand colours and Fire chooses. The accent is the colour that works best as a fill: the most saturated, discounted for being very dark or very light. From a record to a stylesheet

  • Copy theme, beside Copy CSS in the Tweaks panel's footer, copies the panel's look as a record. In code, toTheme() from fire/js/tweaks.js returns the same, and set(fromThemeRecord(record)) applies a record that uses a named palette and type set.
  • A record has no light or dark: the theme is the viewer's choice, so Copy theme leaves it out and Copy CSS's data-theme has no counterpart. Otherwise themeToCss writes exactly the variables Copy CSS writes.
  • A spec's families are written to --fd-font-display, --fd-font-sans and --fd-font-mono, each with its metric-matched fallback face first and the system stack behind, and the fallback @font-face rules come with the stylesheet. The notes name the families your page must load, since Fire ships only its type sets' fonts.
  • contract/looks/ holds a record for each of the fifteen palettes, contract/looks/named/ the eight named looks (Looks), and contract/fire-contract.json lists every token a record sets (tier input) beside the scales and derived tokens.
